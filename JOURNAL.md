# Journal - Mini Mechanical Water Dispenser

## Project Name
Mini Mechanical Water Dispenser

## Project Type
Beginner hardware project - mechanical + electronics

## Main Idea
I wanted to make a small water dispenser using a small DC motor pump, a battery and a switch. The project is simple and the pump is controlled manually by the switch.

---

# Part 1 - Project Idea

**Duration: ~40 minutes**

First I decided to make a small portable water dispenser.

My main idea was to use a small battery powered motor pump to move water. I wanted the project to be simple and easy to use.

I thought about the parts that I would need and how I can connect them together.

### Result
I got the basic idea of the project and decided to make a simple manual dispenser.

---

# Part 2 - Components

**Duration: ~1 hour 15 minutes**

First I made a list of the components needed for the project.

### Components

- Small DC motor pump
- Battery
- ON/OFF switch
- Connecting wires
- Water container

I went to get the parts. The small DC motor pump cost ₹50.

The lithium ion battery I used was a 3.7V 1200mAh battery and it cost ₹300.

The ON/OFF switch cost ₹20. I had to go to another shop because the switch was not available in the first shop.

I also got connecting wires for ₹1.

I used some tape for covering the wires and for safety.

### Result
I collected the main parts needed for the project.

---

# Part 3 - Rough Sketch

**Duration: ~15 minutes**

I made a rough circuit sketch before doing the wiring.

My drawing is not professional, but I used it to understand where the motor, battery, switch and wires should connect.

The basic idea was:

**Battery -> Switch -> DC Motor Pump**

I checked the positive and negative connections before starting the final wiring.

### Result
The rough sketch helped me understand the circuit before assembling it.

---

# Part 4 - Wiring and Assembling

**Duration: ~2 hours**

I collected all the components and started making the circuit.

First, I connected the switch with the positive wire of the DC motor pump. I covered the connection with tape.

After that I connected another wire to the second connection of the switch and covered it with tape for safety.

Then I connected the other wire of the DC motor, which is the negative wire, to the negative terminal of the battery.

I connected the positive wire to the positive terminal of the lithium ion battery.

I checked the wires again and covered the exposed connections with tape.

### Result
The main circuit was assembled.

---

# Part 5 - Problem Faced and Fix

**Duration: ~20 minutes**

I found one problem when I turned on the switch. The DC motor did not work.

I checked the whole wiring system again and found that one wire was not attached properly.

I connected the wire properly and assembled the circuit again.

After checking the wiring, the motor started working.

I also made a few changes to the build. I attached the switch to the battery and covered the wiring with tape.

Because this project is used around water, I tried to cover the electrical connections with tape and keep the wiring protected.

### Result
After fixing the loose connection, the circuit worked properly.

---

# Part 6 - Documentation

**Duration: Actual time spent on documentation**

I also prepared the README file with the project information, components, working and final result.

### Result
The project documentation was completed with the build photos, journal, BOM and README.
